Understanding Social Influence

Social influence happens when other people affect our thoughts, feelings, and behavior. Children ages 10 and under learn from their families, friends, classmates, and surroundings.

Parents play an important role in helping children develop confidence, make healthy decisions, and understand how to treat others.

1. Peer Pressure and Fitting In

Children often want acceptance from their friends. Sometimes they follow others because they fear being left out or rejected.

How parents can help:

  • Teach children to think independently.
  • Encourage friendships based on respect.
  • Practice saying no to uncomfortable situations.
Try this: Ask your child, "What would you do if a friend asked you to do something wrong?"

2. Parents as Role Models

Children observe their parents and often imitate their behavior. The way adults communicate, handle stress, and treat others teaches children important social skills.

How parents can help:

  • Speak respectfully to family members.
  • Show healthy ways to manage anger.
  • Apologize when you make mistakes.
  • Demonstrate kindness and honesty.
Try this: Show your child how to resolve disagreements calmly instead of reacting with anger.

3. Open Communication Builds Confidence

Children need to feel comfortable sharing their experiences and feelings. Open conversations help parents understand what their children face at school and with friends.

How parents can help:

  • Listen without judging.
  • Talk about friendships and school experiences.
  • Encourage children to express their opinions.
  • Remind them they have the right to say no.
Try these phrases:
  • "You are allowed to have your own opinion."
  • "You don't have to follow everyone."
  • "You can always talk to me."
